How to use this page: this is your map, not your workbook — no write-in exercises here. Print it and pin it somewhere visible, or keep it open as a tab. Re-check it every couple of weeks and adjust the dates if life gets in the way; the phases matter more than the exact calendar dates.

Where you are right now

  • telc A1: passed with 91% — strong foundation, no need to revisit basics.
  • A2 → B1 bridge: currently active — connectors, prepositions, modal/reflexive/separable verbs, Perfekt. Mostly in place.
  • Gap to close: everything from solid B1 grammar (Präteritum, Passiv, Relativsätze, Genitiv, Adjektivdeklination, Konjunktiv II) through B2 grammar (Konjunktiv I, advanced Passiv, advanced Konnektoren, Nominalisierung, advanced Relativsätze, Futur/Vermutung) plus exam-task technique for both levels.
  • Input: keep your existing YouTube + workbook routine running throughout — this track is for structured, exam-shaped output practice, not a replacement for input.

telc B2 exam at a glance

PartSkillWhat it tests
LeseverstehenReading3 Teile — detailed comprehension of a long text, matching opinions/statements, matching ads/programs to needs
SprachbausteineReading/LanguageCloze text with multiple-choice gaps — grammar & connectors in context
Schriftlicher AusdruckWritingStructured opinion essay (~200–230 words), Einleitung/Hauptteil/Schluss
HörverstehenListening3 Teile — no hosted audio; texts are read aloud by your browser's own text-to-speech
Mündliche PrüfungSpeakingSeparate module, often bookable independently — not covered in this track yet

Exact task counts and timings shift slightly between official telc test versions — download the current telc B2 Modellsatz PDF from telc.net to confirm before your exam, and register early since B2 slots can fill up.

Weekly rhythm

You're working full-time, so this is built around short weekday sessions plus one longer weekend block — roughly 6–8 hours/week total.
