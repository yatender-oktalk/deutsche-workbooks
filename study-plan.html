telc B2 — Study Plan & Timeline

A1 passed (91%) · target: telc B2, late November 2026 · set your real exam date in the top bar and the timeline below rebuilds around it

How to use this page: this is your map, not your workbook — no write-in exercises here. Print it and pin it somewhere visible, or keep it open as a tab. Re-check it every couple of weeks and adjust the dates if life gets in the way; the phases matter more than the exact calendar dates.

Where you are right now

  • telc A1: passed with 91% — strong foundation, no need to revisit basics.
  • A2 → B1 bridge: currently active — connectors, prepositions, modal/reflexive/separable verbs, Perfekt. Mostly in place.
  • Gap to close: everything from solid B1 grammar (Präteritum, Passiv, Relativsätze, Genitiv, Adjektivdeklination, Konjunktiv II) through B2 grammar (Konjunktiv I, advanced Passiv, advanced Konnektoren, Nominalisierung, advanced Relativsätze, Futur/Vermutung) plus exam-task technique for both levels.
  • Input: keep your existing YouTube + workbook routine running throughout — this track is for structured, exam-shaped output practice, not a replacement for input.

The route: six phases, ~18 weeks

This is the reasoning behind the phases. For dated week-by-week ranges, set your exam date in the top bar — Dein Zeitplan at the top of this page then lays these same six phases out backward from it.

PhaseLengthFocusUse these files
0 — Setup 1 week Confirm your actual exam date & register. Skim this plan. Do a quick self-check: can you read a B1 text without a dictionary? index.html
1 — B1 grammar 4 weeks Close the B1 gap: Präteritum/Perfekt, Passiv, Relativsätze, Genitiv, Adjektivdeklination, Konjunktiv II. ~1.5 workbooks/week. workbooks/A2/workbook-7..8, workbooks/B1/workbook-1..7
2 — B1 exam technique 2 weeks Checkpoint: could you pass a B1 exam today? Timed Leseverstehen, Hörverstehen, Sprachbausteine, and 2–3 letters under real time pressure. exam-prep/B1/*
3 — B2 grammar 6 weeks The real climb: Konjunktiv I/indirekte Rede, Passiv mit Modalverben, fortgeschrittene Konnektoren, Nominalisierung, fortgeschrittene Relativsätze, Futur/Vermutung. ~1 workbook/week. workbooks/B2/workbook-1..6
4 — B2 exam technique 3 weeks Timed B2 Leseverstehen, Hörverstehen and Sprachbausteine sets; write all 3 practice essays under time pressure, then compare against the model answers. Add the Mündliche Prüfung guide + Gesprächstrainer if you're taking the speaking module. exam-prep/B2/*, situations/
5 — Consolidate 2 weeks Redo every cheatsheet as active recall (cover it, rewrite it from memory). Re-run your weakest 2–3 workbooks. One full timed mock. Rest the last 2 days. All cheatsheets, weak-area workbooks

Count backward from your real exam date and keep the phase lengths roughly in proportion — Phase 1 and Phase 3 are the ones to protect if time gets tight; trim Phase 5 last, not first. If there are fewer than ~12 weeks left, Dein Zeitplan shrinks every phase proportionally and flags it as a compressed plan.

Weekly rhythm

You're working full-time, so this is built around short weekday sessions plus one longer weekend block — roughly 6–8 hours/week total.

DaySessionWhat
Mon / Wed45–60 minOne workbook section (not a whole workbook) — write answers, check the key, note anything you got wrong.
Tue / Thu30–45 minInput as usual (YouTube/workbooks) + review yesterday's mistakes from memory before looking them up again.
Fri45–60 minFinish whatever workbook is in progress; skim its cheatsheet once more.
Sat or Sun2–3 hrsThe long block: one full workbook, or one exam-task practice set, done under a timer.
Other weekend dayLightRest, or just re-read a cheatsheet over coffee. Don't skip rest days — they're part of the plan, not a failure to keep it.
